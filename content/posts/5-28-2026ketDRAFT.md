Last year, after a crazy few months to the start of 2025, I decided to take medical leave from my job to focus on my mental health. For the first time in my adult life, those backburner larger than life questions and problems were affectly my day to day health and mood - and subseuqnetly my day to day work. Up until that point, I had been in therapy for many years and was externally extremely stable - but I had the ultimate realization that the way that I treated myself internally just was not fair. 

For various reasons - I've been in and out of therapy for almost my entire life, and I am not doubtful the positive impact is has had on me, and I want to express gratitude for the countless therapists I have had who have helped me through many struggles and off peroids. I also want to acknlowedge the same for the various antidepressants I've used as well (for context: the last time I was on antidepressants was in college; and I stopped right before covid, in January 2020. What a time, woof - but I haven't been on them since).

I say all of this to give some credit to the following: I am no stranger to our current medical system's offerings for those struggling with mental health; or rather, the specific subset that ailed me: depression and anxiety. I'm also familar with the various techniques used to help combat the thought patterns that come with these: here, I'll be addressing common: CBT, or cognitive behavrioal therapy. 

So, when I went on medical leave last year, both of these came to the forefront. Technically, CBT never really went away - I've been doing some form of that implicility for many years - but it did become a more focused effort. But when the thought of meds came back in the picture - I decide to explore something else: ketamine.

Silently, academia has been studying psychedelic substances such as psilocybin for the treatment of mental health disorders since the early 2000s. However, psychedelics being used for mental health treatments has been on a tear within the past decade. For example, in 2016, JHU published a landmark study showing psilocybin's effectiveness against depression and anxiety in cancer patients, and in 2019, the FDA approved the first ketamine-derived medication for a psychiatric indication - the first genuinely new mechanism for depression in decades. Furthermore, since Ketamine is not a 'traditional' psychedelic and has been legal for anesthetic purposes in the US for decades now, off-label use for ketamine to treat depression and anxiety (and now a swath of other mental adjcanet illnesses such as addictions) has also exploded in parallel. And the research is there - we may not know fully why these things work, but for some folks they do; and suprisingly, it worked for me, with few side effects.
